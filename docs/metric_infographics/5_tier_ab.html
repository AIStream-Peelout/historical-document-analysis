Tier A and Tier B · when is an error rate meaningful?

Can two readers agree?

Many Genizah fragments carry more than one text layer: a later hand written over an earlier one, marginalia at right angles, a palimpsest under-text. Any reference for such a page is an editor's selection of one layer plus a linearisation of it. A system that faithfully reads a different layer scores as if it were hallucinating. So before trusting a character error rate on a page, we ask a prior question: did two unrelated systems independently find the reference on it?

1

Two kinds of reader

The test only works because the two readers fail in unrelated ways. One follows the ink and nothing else; the other brings a language prior to the page. Neither can quietly borrow the other's mistakes.

Order-blind line recogniser (Kraken / MiDRASH)Run on segmented lines with no language model. It reads ink, line by line, and cannot invent fluent text: when it fails, the output is garbage, not prose.
Vision-language modelReads the image with a strong language prior. It can reconstruct a damaged word from context — and it can hallucinate a plausible passage that is not on the page at all.

Their error modes are independent. That independence is what makes their agreement informative.

Score each against the reference with the order-blind 5-gram precision

Both readers are scored on the same question — of everything written, how much occurs somewhere on this page — so reading order never decides the outcome. Three real benchmark fragments show the three patterns that appear.

3

Apply the rule

Both conditions must hold on the same page. The seven vision-language models are Gemini Pro, Gemini Flash, Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, HebVL-1.7 and HebVL-1.6; any one of them clearing the cutoff satisfies the second condition.

Ink readerKraken's 5-gram precision ≥ 0.25 — mandatory, no substitute
Language readerat least one of the seven VLMs ≥ 0.25

Kraken is the mandatory half because it is the reader that cannot share a language prior with the reference. Two VLMs agreeing might only mean two models trained on the same canonical text; a line recogniser agreeing means the letters are there, in that shape, on that page.

Why a convergence rule at all

Three numbers behind the split

55 / 131fragments where CER is interpretable at all

Fewer than half the benchmark supports an error-rate headline. The paper reports this as a property of the corpus, not of the models — a Genizah benchmark that quotes CER on all 131 is quoting a number that does not mean the same thing on every page.

0.12 %wrong-page comparisons that reach the 0.25 cutoff

Scoring real outputs against the wrong manuscript gives 78,130 pairs; one in about eight hundred clears 0.25 by accident. Two independent systems both clearing it on the same page by chance is rarer still.

15 of 33high-window, low-alignment outputs that were Kraken reading correctly

Outputs with high 5-gram precision but low aligned precision are the diagnostic case: fifteen of them are the ink reader reading the right ink in a different order — exactly the situation the tier rule is designed to surface rather than punish.

What it does not see

Evidence of agreement, not proof of a reference

Agreement is evidence, not proof. A VLM and an editor can share the same canonical text as a prior — liturgy, Bible — so convergence on such a page is weaker evidence than convergence on a letter or a contract, where there is nothing to remember.
Kraken is mandatory, so segmentation decides eligibility. The 9 fragments where only a VLM finds the reference stay in Tier B even if that VLM is right. A page the line recogniser segments badly cannot be Tier A, whatever else reads it.
Short outputs can clear 0.25 by formula alone. One 27-letter output scored 0.26 on its own page. A minimum evidence length is a possible tightening of the rule.
Four of the 55 are borderline. They qualified only because an over-long HebVL-1.7 output recycled real phrases from the page. Count clipping removes them: 55 → 50.
Tier B pages are not discarded. They carry the precision-family metrics in full, and they are where the multi-layer findings come from — the pages the tier rule excludes from CER are the pages that motivated it.
