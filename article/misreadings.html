The problem

A number that ranks the shape of failure

Character error rate is the standard measure for transcription, and on this material it is close to useless. A model that declines to answer, a model that repeats one word four thousand times, and a model that invents a fluent page of legal Hebrew all produce a CER — and the three numbers are not comparable, because they are not measuring the same thing.

So the scoring pipeline here reports something else first: what the model did. Every transcription is classified into one of four behaviours before any error rate is computed, using thresholds that need no ground truth to apply.

Abstention is honest. Looping is obvious. Hallucination is neither — it is the failure that survives review, and the one that quietly poisons a corpus.

The classifier is deliberately blunt. Under 25 characters, or a refusal phrase, counts as abstained. If a single repeated 12-gram covers 45% or more of the output, that is loop collapse. If fewer than 10% of the output's 5-grams appear anywhere in the ground truth — in any order — the text is not on this page, and it is hallucinated. Everything else is a substantive attempt, and only there does CER get reported.

Order-independence matters more than it sounds. Genizah leaves are palimpsests, margins, and interlinear insertions; the "correct" reading order is an editorial decision. An n-gram precision score does not care about order, so it stays meaningful where CER falls apart, and it answers the only question that matters for hallucination: is this text on the page at all?

Part one

First, the easy corpus

Before the manuscripts, a sanity check on printed text. The Talmud Bavli in the Vilna layout is typeset, high-contrast, and utterly regular — three text bodies per page in three different faces: square type for the gemara, Rashi script for the commentary, a third block for tosafot. If a model cannot read this, it cannot read anything.

Most of them could not read it.

One row in that table deserves a correction rather than a caption. Kraken and Cloud Vision are not scored end-to-end: both read a Vilna page column by column, interleaving the marginal apparatus with the three text bodies, so their flat output is passed to a text LLM that extracts each section before scoring. When that extractor returns nothing, the section scores a CER of exactly 1.0 — and it returned nothing on 80% of Kraken's page-sections and 100% of Cloud Vision's. A number that lands on exactly 1.000 for all 193 sections is not a measurement of reading.

On the 13 pages per section that were processed before the cap hit, Kraken reads gemara at a median CER of 0.041 — respectable, and roughly where Gemini Flash sits. On the two pages where the unsegmented output still survives on disk, Kraken's flat read captures 91% of the ground truth's 5-grams. The engine can read the page. The billing account could not pay for the step that sorts what it read.

Note

What Kraken's 1.0 was actually made of

One engine, one set of pages, five ways of scoring it. The reported 1.0 was a billing artefact; re-running the pipeline with the quota clear puts Kraken at a median 0.040 on gemara at full coverage — better than every frontier API that answered every page, and roughly 9× behind the fine-tuned VLM. The layout-free row is the upper bound on its pure reading: 0.014 when routing is not its problem. Between 0.014 and 0.290 lies everything Kraken does not know about what a Vilna page is.

talmud_paper_long.csv (reported, conditional) · re-run: W&B talmud-kraken-reseg-20260821, 65/65 pages · layout-free figure computed from kraken_flat.txt on the 2 pages that retain it — 105 GT lines of 25+ letters, free end-gap alignment

Two things in that chart deserve suspicion, and the first one turned out to be our fault rather than the models'. Gemini Pro's excellent gemara score is computed over 19 of 65 pages — not because it refused the rest, but because the project's Gemini billing cap was exhausted 21% of the way through the run. Every image upload after that point returned 429 … exceeded its monthly spending cap, 310 times in a row. The benchmark recorded that as missing coverage and moved on.

The same 429 took out the OCR pipeline, which routes its flat text through a Gemini segmentation call: 702 failures, and with them 79% of the section extractions for Kraken and Cloud Vision. Their reported CER of 1.000 is the sound a spending cap makes.

Kraken's row has since been re-run — 2026-08-21, quota restored, 65 of 65 pages, zero failures. It reads the median gemara page at 0.040 rather than 1.000, at full coverage, which puts a plain HTR engine ahead of every frontier API that attempted the whole benchmark. Rashi and Tosafot land at 0.290 and 0.229: the segmenter still struggles to pull the commentary blocks cleanly out of a column-interleaved stream, and it fired its own quality guards 68 times doing it. Cloud Vision's re-run followed on 2026-08-24 and completed the pair: 0.033 on gemara — the best untrained gemara reading in the benchmark — against 0.74 on both Rashi-script sections, where Kraken's Hebrew-trained model keeps a 2.5× edge.

The second is the gap between mean and median — and that gap is the whole argument about metrics.

Part two

Then, the real thing

The Cairo Genizah is not typeset. It is a storeroom's worth of discarded handwriting — legal deeds, letters, shopping lists, children's alphabets — in a dozen hands, on damaged paper and parchment, much of it in Judaeo-Arabic: Arabic language written in Hebrew letters. There is no printing press anywhere in it.

Running the Talmud-trained models on 131 verified Genizah fragments is where the investigation properly begins.

Exhibit 5

The same models, both corpora

Every system degrades — the fine-tuned v1.7 by 81×, Gemini Pro by 31×, Kraken by 10×, Cloud Vision by 19×. With both OCR rows re-run, no line improves any more — the earlier "improvements" were billing artefacts at the printed end, not manuscript competence. And the Genizah end is flattered twice: it is CER conditional on a substantive attempt, and the dot size shows how thin that subset gets — Claude Sonnet 5's 0.43 rests on 22% of the corpus, Kraken's 0.41 on 98%.

talmud_paper_summary.csv (gemara median) → genizah_offline_summary.csv (cer_median_substantive) · log scale · different denominators — see note · systems below 25 substantive attempts held out (base Qwen3-VL n=2, v1.6·1100 n=18)

A drop was expected. What was not expected is where the drop concentrates. Splitting the same 131 fragments by the language of the text turns a general difficulty into a specific one.

Exhibit 6

Hallucination rate by script and language

Every off-the-shelf language-model-bearing system hallucinates more on Judaeo-Arabic than on Hebrew — Claude Sonnet 5 goes from 35% to 78%, Gemini Pro from 17% to 51%. Kraken, which has no language prior to fall back on, invents nothing at all on any of the 82 Judaeo-Arabic fragments — and v2.0a, fine-tuned on this material, now matches it at zero. The failure is not reading the letters; it is what the model does when the letters stop forming words it expects.

genizah_offline_by_script.csv · cell = % of that bucket's fragments classified hallucinated · n shown per cell

This is the mechanism the whole investigation turns on. A vision-language model reading a damaged manuscript is doing two things at once: recognising letterforms, and predicting plausible text. When the letterforms are clear, the two agree. When the ink has faded and the hand is unfamiliar and the language is not the one the prior expects, the second process keeps running without the first — and produces beautiful, well-formed, entirely invented Hebrew.

The model is not lying about what it sees. It has stopped looking.

How literally that turned out to be true

Midway through this work, a check on the shipped adapters found something that made the phrase uncomfortably precise. Every Qwen3-VL adapter through v1.7 carried 108 vision-tower LoRA tensors whose lora_B weights were exactly zero. Unsloth's gradient-checkpointing hook never reached the vision tower, so the gradient stopped at the projector. Every "vision fine-tune" up to that point had trained the language half of the model and nothing else.

No metric caught it. Training loss fell. Validation CER improved. The Talmud benchmark set records. Nothing short of reading the weights themselves could see it — which is why the fix now ships with a script that asserts, tensor by tensor, that the vision LoRA actually moved.

Exhibit 7

The same recipe, with the vision tower actually training

v1.8a and v1.8b are the same data, the same hyperparameters, the same step count. The only difference is whether gradient reached the vision tower. Hallucination falls by more than half and n-gram precision multiplies by 2.6 — the model starts looking, and stops guessing.

Part three

Down at the letter

Strip away abstention, looping and hallucination, and look only at the transcriptions that genuinely tried. What is left is the honest error — and it is not distributed randomly across the alphabet.

Hebrew has several pairs of letters that differ by a stroke or a corner. ד and ר differ by whether the top-right corner is square or rounded. ב and כ differ by a heel. In a printed book, these are unambiguous. In a hurried eleventh-century cursive hand, they routinely are not — and a probe that aligns each transcription against the ground truth can count exactly how often each pair gets swapped.

Exhibit 8

Confusable-pair swap rates: manuscript hand vs printed type

Corpus

The identical model swaps ד/ר on 6.65% of occurrences in manuscript hands and 0.036% in printed type — a factor of 185. Across all twenty tracked pairs the gap is 36×. Hebrew is not the hard part; handwriting is.

confusable_pairs.csv · alignment-based substitution counts, pooled within corpus · rate = swaps ÷ occurrences of either letter

Kraken is the quiet winner here too. Pooled across the twenty tracked pairs on Genizah material it swaps at 1.58%, against 1.83% for the best vision-language model and 2.60% for the generation before it. An engine with no idea what the words mean discriminates letterforms slightly better than one that does — and when it fails, it fails visibly, into nonsense rather than into fluency.

Which is the practical conclusion of the whole investigation. The language prior that makes a VLM good at printed Talmud is the same faculty that makes it dangerous on a damaged Judaeo-Arabic deed. The two cannot be separated by prompting, and they were not separated by fine-tuning until the vision tower was genuinely in the loop.

Provenance

What the benchmark had to survive first

None of the above means anything if the ground truth is wrong. Published Genizah transcriptions are editions, not OCR targets: they carry reconstructions of lost text in brackets, they sometimes describe a different leaf than the image attached to them, and a scraping error can duplicate a passage so that a perfect transcription scores as half wrong.

Exhibit 9

From 5,714 candidate documents to 131 admissible fragments

Fourteen fragments needed duplicated ground truth repaired; nineteen were dropped outright because no system's reading overlapped the transcription enough to believe they described the same leaf. The survivors are disjoint from every training split, and each one's editorial reconstructions are rendered as gaps rather than as text a model is expected to invent.

Method. All scores are recomputed offline from each system's raw saved output against repaired ground truth, so a correction to the ground truth never requires re-running a model. Failure-mode thresholds: abstention below 25 characters or on a refusal phrase; loop collapse at a repeated-12-gram coverage of 0.45; hallucination below 0.10 order-independent 5-gram precision. CER is reported only for substantive attempts and never as a bare mean.

Caveats. Coverage differs by system, and not always for reasons that reflect the model. Genuine failures — decode-time token caps, timeouts, recitation blocks — are deliberately counted as failures here, since a model that cannot finish a page has failed it; a truncated generation is kept and scored rather than dropped. A billing cap is not that. In the 2026-07-13 run an exhausted Gemini spending cap produced 1,015 of the 1,084 recorded failures: it cost Gemini Pro roughly 35 of 65 pages outright and degraded 30 more, and it destroyed the Kraken and Cloud Vision section scores outright, since their pipeline routes through a Gemini segmentation call. Kraken (2026-08-21) and Gemini Flash (2026-08-23, now 98.5–100% coverage) have since been re-run with the cap clear, and Cloud Vision followed on 2026-08-24; only Gemini Pro remains pending. GPT-5.6 Sol's 30.8% is neither — it is a deliberate 20-page sample. Three different things, one coverage column. Talmud and Genizah CER are not the same denominator and the cross-corpus exhibit is a directional comparison, not a like-for-like one. The unseen-font generalisation probe was still running when these figures were frozen and is not reported here.
